Design Proposal · For Review

DCM Heritage

A third slab-label design built print-first for consumer inkjet and laser printers: certificate typography on ivory, one saturated brand band, and a solid grade chip. Rendered at exact 2.8″ × 0.8″ proportions, 500 DPI.

Today, for contrast: DCM Modern

Screen-first · prints poorly2.8″ × 0.8″ · ~92% ink coverage
  • The full-bleed dark gradient is ~92% ink coverage: it bands on inkjets, dries muddy purple-brown on cheap CMYK, curls thin paper, and lays enough toner on lasers to gloss and flake at the cut line.
  • Great on screen — this stays as-is. Heritage is an alternative, not a replacement.

Variant A — Heritage, base

Proposed default~9% ink coverage
  • Ivory ground (#faf8f4) with near-black ink text — nothing on this label can band or bleed.
  • The purple band + hairline gold pinline is the brand signature at slab distance, the way BGS reads black-gold and CGC reads blue. Small solid areas print crisply on both printer types.
  • Letterspaced caps context line is the certificate cue that makes a light label read premium instead of plain.
  • Serial set in monospace — reads as an authentic document number, and aligns digit-for-digit across a case of slabs. The DCM logo mark anchors the bottom-right on every variant.

Variant B — customized with card colors

Customization: card-extracted band~9% ink coverage
  • The band and grade chip take the card's own extracted palette (here a teal→orange Charizard) — the existing Card Colors system, applied to the band instead of the whole background.
  • Gradient lives only inside the 0.07″ band, so inkjet banding physically can't show.

Variant C — customized with a pattern band + Gem Mint

Customization: pattern band · gold 10~11% ink coverage
  • Your existing geometric patterns (mosaic shown) render inside the band — full customization vocabulary, kept in the one place a consumer printer can hold detail.
  • Grade 10 flips the chip to gold with a keyline — the Gem Mint label becomes visibly special in a case of slabs, using the grade-color config that already shipped.

Back label — verify, sub-grades, emblems

Back of every Heritage label~13% ink coverage · QR scannable at 0.5″
  • QR stays black-on-white with a bordered quiet zone — the one element that must never be styled, and the easiest thing to ruin with a dark background. This QR is real and scans to the verify page.
  • Grade recap + all four sub-grades in the center, with the serial repeated so front and back are independently identifying.
  • Emblems as outlined chips (Founder ★ and VIP ◆ shown — your max-2 rule). Outlines instead of solid fills keep them crisp on inkjet and echo the certificate style; Card Lovers ♥ renders in the same chip shape in rose.
  • The DCM logo mark anchors the bottom-right of both faces — navy prints reliably on both printer types.

Open questions for you

Band position — left edge (shown) or full-width top rule?
Left reads best in a standard slab slot; top reads better if labels are often photographed straight-on for listings.
Grade-tier chip colors — gold for 10 only, or a full tier ramp (gold 10 / silver 9 / bronze 8)?
Gold-only keeps the hierarchy sharpest; a ramp sells subgrade pride but dilutes the 10.
"DCM GRADING" wordmark vs. the actual logo mark in the bottom-right?
The real logo needs a monochrome variant to stay print-safe at this size.
Background choices — lock to a curated light set (white / ivory / cool gray / blush) as recommended, or allow any color with an ink-coverage warning?
