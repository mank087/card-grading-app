# The DCM Grading Standard

**Version 1.1** · Effective 31 August 2026 · Engine `DCM_Grading_v9.23`

Published by Dynamic Collectibles Management LLC.

---

## 1. What this document is

This is the standard DCM grades to. It is our own, it is published here in full,
and it is versioned, so that any grade we issue can be checked against the rules
that produced it.

It is written to be precise enough that a reader who understands it can predict
what DCM will do with a given card. If you can read this document and still not
know why a card scored what it scored, that is a defect in the document and we
want to hear about it.

### Calibration

We calibrate this standard against the published grading criteria of PSA, BGS,
CGC and SGC. Where their public rubrics define a condition, our ladder rows are
written to land in the same place.

We do not claim a DCM grade will equal the grade one of those companies would
assign. They do not publish their internal thresholds, their graders apply
judgment we cannot observe, and any two of them routinely disagree with each
other about the same card. Claiming equivalence to something unpublished would
not be a standard, it would be a guess with a brand name attached.

What we do claim is narrower and testable: our criteria are public, our grades
follow from them, and both are versioned so a change is visible.

## 2. The scale

Whole numbers, 1 through 10. No decimals, no half grades.

| Grade | Name | Profile |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Gem Mint | No confirmed handling defects. Minor manufacturing variance is acceptable. |
| 9 | Mint | One to three minor defects, visible only on close inspection. |
| 8 | Near Mint-Mint | One moderate defect, or several minor defects across categories. |
| 7 | Near Mint | Multiple moderate defects. |
| 6 | Excellent-Mint | Widespread moderate wear, or one isolated heavy defect. |
| 5 | Excellent | Heavy wear beginning to dominate. |
| 4 | Very Good-Excellent | Clear structural or heavy surface damage. |
| 3 | Very Good | Heavy rounding, tearing, or severe whitening. |
| 2 | Good | Severe damage or material loss. |
| 1 | Poor | Destroyed, missing pieces, or miscut beyond recognition. |

A 10 requires genuine scrutiny. Most cards show at least minor wear.

---

## 3. The four categories

Every card is scored in four categories: **Centering**, **Corners**, **Edges**,
and **Surface**. Each is scored 1 to 10 on the front and on the back
independently.

**A category's score is the lower of its two faces.** A card with pristine front
corners and a chipped back corner does not average to something comfortable. It
takes the back corner's score.

---

## 4. How each category is scored

### 4.1 The visibility test

Every category except Centering is scored by how noticeable a defect is, not by
invented millimetre measurements. Three bands:

- **Close inspection only.** You have to look for it, at an angle or magnified.
- **Noticeable at normal viewing distance.** Someone holding the card sees it
  without hunting.
- **Obvious.** It is the first thing you see.

Severity describes visibility. It does not carry its own deduction. Scores come
from the ladders below and nowhere else.

### 4.2 Centering

Centering is the one category that is **measured, not judged**. The two opposing
border widths are compared and a ratio computed. If a ratio was not derived from
two measured widths, it is not a measurement, and the axis is reported as not
measurable rather than estimated.

| Ratio (worse axis, front) | Score |
|---|---|
| 55/45 or better | 10 |
| 56/44 to 60/40 | 9 |
| 61/39 to 65/35 | 8 |
| 66/34 to 70/30 | 7 |
| 71/29 to 80/20 | 6 |
| 81/19 to 85/15 | 5 |
| 86/14 to 90/10 | 4 |
| 91/9 to 95/5 | 3 |
| Worse than 95/5 | 2 |
| Miscut | 1 |

Back centering is held to a looser threshold than front, consistent with market
practice.

**The boundary resolves downward.** Photo measurement carries about ±2% of
imprecision. That figure describes the accuracy of the measurement, not a wider
band of acceptable centering. Where the measurement cannot establish which side
of 55/45 a card falls on, it scores 9, not 10.

This is deliberate. See section 8.

> **Known limitation, stated plainly.** The confidence figure in section 7
> reports how much of the card we could see. It does not currently distinguish
> "the photo was clear but this measurement was borderline" from "the photo was
> clear and the measurement was decisive." Both report the same confidence. We
> resolve borderline cases downward rather than papering over them, but a reader
> should know that the confidence figure is about visibility, not about how close
> a call the grade was.

### 4.3 Corners

Scored on all eight corners, four per face, each examined individually.

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 10 | All corners sharp. Zero fiber exposure or softening even on close inspection. |
| 9 | One corner with a pinpoint of whitening or slight softening, close inspection only. |
| 8 | Minor close-inspection wear on two or more corners. Not noticeable at normal distance. |
| 7 | Whitening or softening plainly noticeable at normal distance on one or two corners. Points still defined. |
| 6 | Noticeable wear on multiple corners, or obvious blunting on one. |
| 5 | Widespread moderate wear with early rounding. |
| 4 | Clear rounding. One or more corners no longer pointed. |
| 3 | Heavy rounding across corners. |
| 2 | Severe damage. Corners nearly circular, or material loss. |
| 1 | Corner or corners missing. |

### 4.4 Edges

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 10 | All edges clean. Zero whitening even on close inspection. Smooth factory cut. |
| 9 | One tiny white fleck, close inspection only. |
| 8 | Two or three tiny flecks, or isolated minor roughness. Not noticeable at normal distance. |
| 7 | Whitening plainly noticeable along part of one edge, or a single tiny chip. |
| 6 | Noticeable whitening on multiple edges, continuous whitening along much of one edge, or a clear chip. |
| 5 | Heavy whitening across substantial portions, or multiple chips. |
| 4 | Whitening along most edge length, or heavy chipping throughout. |
| 3 | Nearly continuous whitening, or severe chipping and material loss. |
| 2 | Complete edge wear or major material loss. |
| 1 | Edge destroyed or delaminated. |

Roughness and whitening are different defects. A card can have a rough cut with
no white showing.

### 4.5 Surface

Scored across both faces on a systematic nine-zone grid, eighteen zones total.

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 10 | No confirmed handling defects. A single manufacturing print dot under 0.3mm in a non-critical area is acceptable. |
| 9 | One minor close-inspection flaw: a hairline scratch visible only at an angle, one or two tiny white dots, or a trace mark. |
| 8 | Two or more minor close-inspection flaws, a light scratch under 1cm, a small print defect, three to four white dots, or minor gloss loss. |
| 7 | A scratch, mark, or print defect plainly noticeable at normal distance. Five or more white dots. Moderate gloss loss or indentation. |
| 6 | Multiple noticeable defects, large print defect, heavy gloss loss, small stain, noticeable holo disruption. |
| 5 | Clear scratching, noticeable defects on both faces, medium stain, severe gloss loss. |
| 4 | Deep scratch into the coating, heavy scratching, large or multiple stains. |
| 3 | Severe surface damage across the card. |
| 2 | Extensive damage, delamination, or heavy staining. |
| 1 | Surface destroyed. |

### 4.6 Full-art and borderless cards

Centering is a measurement of border widths. A card printed without a border has
no border widths to compare, and there is nothing there to be off-centre.

**A card with no border is not judged off-centre.** Where a face carries no
border on either axis, centering for that face is scored 10 and reported as
*Centered* rather than as a ratio. We do not substitute the art, the frame, or a
design element for a border and then measure that: a printed element is not a cut
edge, and treating it as one would invent a deduction the card cannot have earned.

Where a card is bordered on one axis and full-bleed on the other, only the
bordered axis is measured. The full-bleed axis is reported as not applicable, not
as a failed measurement.

This applies to full-art, borderless, extended-art and full-bleed printings
across every category we grade.

### 4.7 Autographs

**An autograph is never a surface defect, and it never replaces a numeric grade.**

A signature is ink that someone deliberately put on the card. It is not handling
damage, and it is not a manufacturing flaw. Scoring it as either would be a
category error, and one that used to cost signed cards several points.

A signed card receives a **full numeric grade**: all four categories are scored,
and surface is scored **as if the ink were absent**. We judge the stock, the
gloss, and the finish around and beneath the strokes, exactly as we would on the
same card unsigned. A scratch under a signature is still a scratch. The signature
itself is not.

What differs is the designation, not the grade:

- **Manufacturer-authenticated autographs** — a certification hologram, a
  "Certified Autograph Issue" line, or an on-card authenticity claim from the
  manufacturer — are a **feature of the card**. The grade stands on its own with
  no additional notation.
- **Unverified autographs** — a signature with no on-card claim of authenticity —
  receive their **full numeric grade** and carry the notation
  **"Altered - Unverified Autograph"** on the grade record and on the label.

The notation says what we can and cannot establish from a photograph: that the
card is signed, and that we are not authenticating the signature. It is a
statement about provenance, not a condition penalty, and it does not lower any
subgrade.

Handwriting that is not a signature — a name, a date, a price, an inventory mark —
is a post-production alteration, caught by the pre-grading verification in
section 9, not by this rule.

## 5. The final grade: weakest link

**The final grade is the lowest of the four category scores.**

```
Final grade = MIN(centering, corners, edges, surface)
```

A card with Centering 9, Corners 8, Edges 9, Surface 7 grades **7**. It is
limited by Surface. There is no averaging and no weighting.

We state this plainly because it is stricter than some other graders, who allow
an overall grade to sit above the lowest subgrade. We do not. A card is as good
as its worst aspect, because that is the aspect a buyer will notice first.

### Structural damage caps

Structural damage overrides everything else, however clean the rest of the card.
When more than one cap applies, the lowest wins.

| Damage | Maximum grade |
|---|---|
| Crease or fold with colour break | 4 |
| Corner lift or tilt | 4 |
| Heavy crease, or multiple creases | 3 |
| Tear or rip | 3 |
| Missing piece | 2 |

```
Final = MIN(structural cap, lowest subgrade)
```

## 6. What does not count against a card

- **Manufacturing variance.** Factory-rounded corners on trading card stock,
  print dots, roller marks, and centering that left the cutting machine that way
  are characteristics of the card, not handling damage.
- **Image artifacts.** Marks visible only at extreme magnification that could be
  compression noise do not affect scores.
- **Poor photography.** Blur, glare and shadow lower our stated confidence. They
  never lower a grade. A card is not penalised for being photographed badly.

## 7. Confidence and uncertainty

Every grade carries a confidence letter based on how much of the card we could
actually see.

| Letter | Visibility | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| A | 95 to 100% | ±0 |
| B | 85 to 94% | ±1 |
| C | 70 to 84% | ±2 |
| D | Under 70% | ±3 |

Uncertainty is a reliability indicator, not a deduction. A grade of 8 with ±1
means our reading of the card is 8 and better photographs could move it a point
in either direction. It does not mean the card is a 7.

A C or D means we recommend new photographs.

---

## 8. Which way we lean when we are unsure

**Against overgrading.**

Where a card sits genuinely on a boundary and the evidence cannot settle it, we
take the lower grade and say so through the confidence figure.

The reasoning is asymmetric harm. An undergraded card disappoints its owner
immediately, and they can ask us to look again. An overgraded card disappoints
whoever buys it, months later, at the point of sale, when the correction is
expensive and the trust is already spent.

## 9. How a grade is produced

DCM Optic™ evaluates every card as follows.

1. **Pre-grading verification.** Authenticity, alterations, trimming, holder
   detection, image completeness.
2. **Image quality assessment.** Establishes the confidence letter in section 7.
3. **Three independent evaluations.** The card is graded three separate times.
4. **Median consensus.** The middle of the three grades becomes the basis for
   the result. We take the median rather than the average deliberately: an
   average lets a single outlier pull the result, a median does not.
5. **Magnified inspection.** Segmented crops of corners, edges and surface
   regions are examined at higher magnification, with multiple votes per region.
6. **Corroboration.** Findings that would significantly lower a grade have to be
   supported by more than one observation:
   - Structural damage requires a majority of the three evaluations to agree, or
     confirmation from the magnified inspection.
   - A magnified finding that would pull a category well below the main
     evaluation's reading requires either a second affected region or agreement
     from the main evaluation.
7. **Final calculation.** Weakest link and structural caps, per section 5.

### On corroboration, precisely

Corroboration applies to structural damage and to magnified findings that would
substantially lower a category. It is not a blanket rule that every defect must
appear in two of three evaluations. Ordinary defects observed in the evaluation
that sets the consensus are reflected in the grade.

We state this precisely because a looser claim would be easy to write and would
not be true.

## 10. Versioning

This standard is versioned independently of the grading engine.

- **Standard version** identifies the rules on this page.
- **Engine version** identifies the implementation that applied them. It appears
  on every grading report.

A change to any threshold, ladder row, or cap is a version increment and is
recorded in the change log below. Grades are not retroactively restated when the
standard changes. A report shows the standard and engine that produced it.

### Change log

**v1.1 — 31 August 2026.** Adds section 4.6, full-art and borderless cards: a
card with no border is not judged off-centre (engine `DCM_Grading_v9.22`). Adds
section 4.7, autographs: an autograph is never a surface defect and never
replaces a numeric grade; manufacturer-authenticated signatures are a feature,
and unverified signatures receive a full numeric grade carrying the notation
"Altered - Unverified Autograph" (engine `DCM_Grading_v9.23`). No other threshold
changes.

**v1.0 — 26 August 2026.** First publication as a standalone versioned standard.
Consolidates the criteria previously held only in the grading engine. No
threshold changes from engine `DCM_Grading_v9.19`.

## 11. Scope

This standard covers Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering, sports, Lorcana, One Piece,
Yu-Gi-Oh!, Star Wars, and other trading cards graded from photographs.

Grading from photographs has limits, and this document is explicit about them:
what we cannot see, we say we cannot see, through the confidence letter and
through marking an axis or region as not assessable. We do not fill a gap in the
evidence with a plausible number.
