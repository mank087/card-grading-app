DCM Grading is an AI trading-card grading platform. A collector photographs a card front and back; the platform identifies it, grades it (centering, corners, edges, surface, with a weakest-link final grade 1-10), writes a condition report, prices it from real sold listings, generates a printable slab label, and can list it to eBay. Web is the primary product; iOS and Android apps have near parity.

The single most important thing to understand: the grading engine is a stack of stochastic vision-model calls whose behaviour is governed as much by a 5,437-line prompt as by code. Nearly every serious bug in this project's history has been a perception or prompt problem, not a logic problem, and the defence against them is the anchor harness plus measured evidence — never intuition.

03The grading engine

One grade fires roughly five independent stochastic model calls. Understanding this pipeline is the job.

  1. Geometry gate — one vision call returns frame-fill % and a corner quad (0-1000 normalised) per face. Decides whether zoom crops are image-relative or card-relative.
  2. Main ensemble — a single OpenAI call with n=3 completions over the full rubric. Server takes a median per category, not the model's own summary.
  3. Zoom inspection — the card is cropped into ~28-40 corner/edge/surface regions at native resolution and inspected in parallel batches of ≤8, 5 samples each, majority-voted. Findings can only lower scores.
  4. Structural verification — any crease/bend claim gets a separate temp-0, n=3 verification call with an evidence enum before it is allowed to cap the grade at 4.
  5. Server-side consensus and caps — weakest link (final = MIN of subgrades), rigid-case exclusions, Gem-Mint majority gate, uncertainty gate, then gradeNarrator.ts writes the customer-facing summary after all mutations.
Why narrate-after-consensus exists: before v9.1 the model's prose was written against its own pre-mutation numbers, so a card capped to 6 could carry a summary saying "surfaces are clean". Every number in the final summary is now generated from the post-cap values. If you add a new cap, feed it into the narrator or you will recreate that bug.

Measured properties

  • n=3 is load-bearing. Measured on 2,695 cards: n=1 would flip 11.7% of grades and disable the Gem-Mint unanimity gate. Do not "optimise" it away.
  • Attention dilutes with image count. One crop detects a test defect 3/3; ≤10 crops 3/3; 20 crops 2/3; 24-40 crops in a single call 0/5. This is why zoom batches are capped at 8 and why "just put the crops in the main call" has already lost an A/B twice.
  • Image detail: detail:'high' beat 'original' (Aug 21 A/B) — full-res nearly doubled run-to-run spread for +9% cost. OpenAI downscales anyway: a 3,000px upload reaches the model at roughly 768px on the short side.
  • Repeatability on the anchor set is currently spread 0.00 with 10/11 within tolerance.

The anchor harness — your safety net

scripts/repeatability-harness.ts grades 12 golden calibration cards (scripts/calibration-set.json) N times and reports grade spread, accuracy within tolerance, and must-detect hits. Run it before and after any prompt or engine change. Current baseline: 10/11 within tolerance, spread 0.00, 11/11 stable. Each anchor exists because a real customer complaint or bug produced it — the Xerosic must detect whitening, the Pineiro must detect a crease, the Gengar must not hallucinate one.

Known bad anchor: the 1991 Fleer Jordan (expected 10) has graded 8 on every version tested. It is photographed white-border-on-light-desk (card/background contrast of 2 out of 255, the worst in the set) and the design has an intentionally thicker bottom band. Nothing can measure its centering. Treat its failure as expected, not as a regression, until the card is rephotographed.

How labels render

The single source of truth for Heritage is HeritageLabelPreview (SVG) plus heritageLayout.ts geometry. Everything else composites from it: heritageSlabPdfDoc.tsx (react-pdf, for print), heritageRaster.ts (browser canvas), heritageSlabGenerator.tsx, cardImageGenerator.ts (eBay/social images), the report component, and the mobile SlabCard.

The org's design document lives at organizations.storefront.slab.design and is read only through resolveOrgLabelDesign(); legacy flat keys are always written alongside it via designToLegacySlab for backwards compatibility.

Run scripts/label-design-snapshot.ts check after any Heritage renderer change. It hashes 16 fixtures (SVG + PDF) and is the only thing standing between a small geometry tweak and silently breaking every printed label. The PDF normaliser strips /ID and date objects so hashes are stable.

Known react-pdf gotchas: rgba() is unsupported (use hex + opacity), and the fold-over label needs edge-aware bleeds. Grade colour was hardcoded purple in seven files at one point — check for stragglers before assuming a colour prop is respected.

07Mobile apps

iOS and Android, Expo SDK 54, near-parity with web. Runtime version 1.0.2 is live in both stores (since 5 Aug 2026).

OTA publishing rules, learned the hard way. Publish at 1.0.2 and at the legacy lanes (iOS 1.0.1, Android 1.0.0) until old installs age out. Never omit --platform. And never OTA master to Android blind: the credits page has a platform fork (iOS StoreKit vs Android Stripe-in-WebView) and pushing the wrong bundle breaks purchasing or violates store policy.

Purchase architecture

Credit packs are consumables (dcm.credits.{basic,pro,elite,vip}). Apple guideline 3.1.1 forbids exposing restorePurchases() for consumables, so iOS uses native StoreKit and Android uses Stripe inside a WebView (Google-approved). The fork is in pages/credits.tsx. Server verification is /api/iap/apple/verify and /api/iap/google/verify, with webhooks for each; transactions land in iap_transactions.

Other mobile facts

  • All Firebase Analytics, Meta App Events and ATT tracking were removed 20 Jul 2026. That was a native change: it needs an EAS rebuild and store resubmit, not an OTA. Sentry and Facebook login were preserved.
  • Apple Sign In web JWT secret expires 1 Nov 2026 and must be regenerated.
  • Apple Developer Program is enrolled as an Individual; conversion to Organization is deferred until after the Android launch.
  • Mobile card detail does not use the shared CardSlab component the way web does — slab markup is inlined in eight web detail clients, which is why parity bugs recur.

08Enterprise / white-label

Driven by card shops wanting to grade under their own brand. First customer: Apex Grading (launch 28 Aug 2026). Strategy is co-brand, never fully white-label — the store's logo leads on labels, reports and the verification page, but DCM remains the named engine.

Phase 1 shipped 14 Aug (Dealer $199/400, Enterprise $399/1,000, payer scope, storefronts), followed by an audit and fix pass on 17 Aug that required two migrations applied in order (billing index first). Each org gets a storefront at /enterprise/{slug}, branded slab labels, a serial registry and branded card pages.

Open: the embeddable verification widget (the app currently sends no frame-ancestors headers, so embedding is blocked), and Modern-style print parity in the Label Designer, which was excluded on purpose.

09Pricing, credits, subscriptions

Credits are deducted through /api/stripe/deduct, which is idempotent — that was added after the July duplicate-charge incident. Client-side submit locks were added at the same time.

Market pricing has two paths: sports uses the SportsCardsPro/PriceCharting API (a 6.1M-row local mirror was imported in July then dropped on 4 Jul because it blew the Supabase storage budget — the code degrades to the API automatically via SPORTS_LOCAL_DB_ENABLED); everything else falls back to eBay Browse API active listings, which is why non-sports pricing is labelled an "asking range" rather than a sold price. dcmEstimate.ts interpolates between graded tiers with a 0.35-0.70 market multiplier.

Card Lovers renewals are idempotent and reconciled; a July review found the upgrade route 100% broken (two stacked bugs) and fixed it. One policy decision remains open on upgrade credit maths.

10Card identification & databases

The model reads the card, then the route looks it up in our own database and, on a match, overwrites set name, number, year and rarity — sometimes the name too. validated_source records which table did it.

The identity rule, added 25 Aug 2026: a force_regrade must refresh the condition grade only, never the identity. src/lib/grading/preserveIdentity.ts enforces this across all eight routes; pass ?reidentify=true to opt back in. This exists because a regrade renamed two customers' cards ("Kewl Tune Synchro" → "Aromalilith Magnolia") when a misread set code hit a real but different printing.

The matching rule that follows from it: the printed name beats the number. A set-code or collector-number hit whose name disagrees is never accepted. That guard now exists in Yu-Gi-Oh, MTG, Lorcana, Star Wars and Naruto; One Piece and Sports already had it. Pokémon and the generic TCG matcher still use a weak 5-character prefix test (open item).

Other traps: Scryfall rejects default HTTP user agents (send a custom UA or the MTG database silently goes stale); the Pokémon route fires a background /api/pokemon/verify after every grade that can rewrite identity in a second UPDATE; cards.user_condition_report ships in two incompatible shapes, so always optional-chain it.

15Landmines

Production database. Never scan whole tables. Never bulk-select heavy JSON columns (conversational_grading, pokemon_api_data, cv_centering). Page in small batches with a delay, select only the columns you need, and stop immediately on 522s. This took the site down on 8 Aug 2026.

  • Do not delete "detection-pressure" prompt lines. The v9.4 Phase-1 attempt removed three "when in doubt, treat as a defect" lines and accuracy fell to 68%, with a creased card graded 9. They were compensating for blind zoom crops. They were restored verbatim and only coexist with 91% accuracy because the perception underneath was fixed first.
  • Do not retry the v9.6 "corners diet" naively. Removing per-corner prose reproducibly regressed the vintage-texture false-positive anchor. The prose is load-bearing.
  • Do not put all zoom crops in the main call. Measured 0/5 detection. It has lost this A/B twice.
  • Eight routes, not one. Any grading-route change usually needs all eight category routes.
  • Windows/Bash quirks: patching regex text through a Bash heredoc silently eats backslashes (\s became s and shipped). Use the file-edit tools for anything containing escapes.
  • gpt-5.6-luna rejects temperature and top_p, and reasoning tokens consume max_completion_tokens — a 250-token budget returns empty content. Budget generously.
  • Orphan-user backfill is deliberately deferred. Do not re-propose it.
